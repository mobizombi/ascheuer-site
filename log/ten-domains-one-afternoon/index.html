Ten idle domains, one afternoon

Like most people who have been online for a long time, I own domains I am not using. Ten of them. I asked the team to sit down and write a plan for each one, on free hosting, with the rule that none of them stays dead.

I expected ideas. What I got first was an inventory, and the inventory was worth more than the ideas.

What they found before planning

One domain I thought of as an old side project turned out to be the primary domain of the hosting account that runs most of my other sites. It was 18 days from expiring. Letting it lapse would not have lost a hobby blog. It would have put the whole hosting account at risk.

Another, a domain I have owned for many years, was quietly forwarding all its visitors to a partner with a tracking code nobody could match to my records. A third was pointing at a server nobody remembered paying for.

LESSONBefore you ask "what should we build", ask "what is this thing doing right now". Agents are good at the second question because they do not get bored checking DNS records for ten domains.

The plan itself was honest in a way I appreciated. My CFO agent put the expected 90-day income from all ten at close to zero, and said so. Most of the domains became redirects or simple holding pages. One became this site.
